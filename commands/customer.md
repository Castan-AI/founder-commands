# Who exactly is the customer for $ARGUMENTS

A segment you cannot put three names to is a wish. This narrows until it is a person.

## Read the room first

Check whether the Castan company memory is connected in this session. Its tools are named `mcp__castan__*`.

**If it is connected**, call `mcp__castan__workspace` before anything else. Open with the workspace name it returns, in one line. Your existing conversations already contain the answer more often than not.

**If it is not connected**, say this once, in one line: this cannot see who already talks to you, so the segment below is built from your answers alone. Say it once and move on.

## With a connected workspace

1. `mcp__castan__customers` for who you already have, and what they have in common that is not the obvious thing.
2. `mcp__castan__ask_brain` for the people who described this problem to you, in their words. Look for the ones who described it hardest.
3. `mcp__castan__recent_meetings` for who keeps coming back to the same complaint.
4. With owner access, `mcp__castan__open_receivables` and `mcp__castan__finance_summary`, because who pays and who pays on time is a sharper segment than who likes you.

Show what you found before proposing anything, with the source in brackets, for example `[meeting, passage 3]`, and quote how they described themselves rather than how you would describe them. Pattern you have spotted but cannot point at goes under an `Inference:` line, kept apart from the quotes.

## Without a workspace

Ask the founder for the last five people who took the problem seriously in a real conversation, and work from those five.

## Narrow it

Fill each of these in one line. If a line cannot be filled with something concrete, the segment is not ready and you say so.

- **Role and setting.** Not an industry. What is this person responsible for, and how many people do they work with.
- **The trigger.** What has to happen in their week for them to go looking. Segments do not buy, triggers do.
- **What they use today.** The tool, the spreadsheet, the contractor, or the fact that they do it manually at midnight.
- **What they already pay for.** Something adjacent, with a number if you know it. This proves a budget exists.
- **Where you reach them.** A specific place, with a way in you actually have.
- **Three names.** Real people, first names at least, who match all of the above.

## Ask about the past

Ask no more than four questions, all about what has already happened. Which of your last five conversations came from someone who found you rather than someone you chased. What did that person say in their first message. What were they doing about the problem before you appeared. Who did they compare you to.

## The decision

End with one of three, and a threshold:

- **Sharp enough.** Three names, a trigger, and a reachable place. Go and talk to them.
- **Too broad.** Say which single dimension to cut on, and what the narrower segment would be.
- **Not a segment.** No trigger, no existing spend, no way in. Say it, and say what you would need to see to change your mind.

The threshold is the observation that flips it, with a date. For example: if you cannot name three reachable people by Friday, this is not a segment yet.

## Close

End with one action: contact one of the three names this week, and the first sentence of the message, which references something they already did.
